B. Style Definition (prose, ≤120 words)  
A one-colour sports illustration style combining observational hand-inked drawing with the imperfect finish of a physical screenprint. The supplied photograph controls the player’s identity, expression, pose, action, uniform and composition. Organic, pressure-sensitive ink marks describe anatomy, fabric and movement without becoming cartoon-like. Facial likeness is retained through accurate proportions, distinctive landmarks, natural asymmetry and selective internal detail, all expressed through flat ink and negative space. Contours are irregular, broken and human, with no mechanically straight or vector-clean edges. Ink fill is uneven, grainy and occasionally starved. Jersey patterns, sponsor marks and club crests remain recognisable. The result feels handmade, athletic and specific to the photographed player.

E. Art Director Amendments  
SOURCE IMAGE IS THE AUTHORITY  
This is a universal image-to-image treatment. Do not impose a running pose, ball-carrying action, fend, celebration or any other predetermined sporting gesture. Preserve the exact action, posture, expression, framing and equipment visible in each supplied player photograph.  
The style may transform how the player is rendered, but it must not transform who the player is or what they are doing.  
HAND-INKED FIRST, SCREENPRINTED SECOND  
The illustration should feel as though an artist carefully observed the photograph and described it using expressive ink, before the artwork was transferred to a screen and printed imperfectly.  
Do not interpret “cut” as rigid, geometric, angular or faceted. The shapes can have decisive edges, but their construction must remain organic and responsive to anatomy, fabric, movement and likeness.  
The finish should combine:  
1. Observational hand-inked drawing  
2. Simplified one-colour shape construction  
3. Imperfect physical screenprint reproduction  
The screenprint texture must never replace the quality of the underlying drawing.  
ORGANIC LINEWORK  
No contour should feel mechanically generated. Lines must vary naturally in thickness and pressure. They can swell, thin, hesitate, overlap, taper irregularly, break and resume.  
Nominally straight elements such as jersey stripes, seams and logo edges should remain recognisable but carry slight hand movement. They must not become ruler-straight, geometrically perfect or vector-clean.  
Use:  
* Naturally wobbling contours  
* Imperfect joins  
* Broken or dry line endings  
* Small changes in pressure  
* Occasional ink pooling  
* Slightly frayed edges  
* Expressive internal marks  
* Irregular but intentional negative-space cuts  
Avoid random scribbling. Every mark should help describe identity, anatomy, fabric, action or light.  
FACIAL IDENTITY MUST SURVIVE  
The face must remain recognisable as the exact person in the reference photograph. Do not replace the face with a generic illustrated athlete or a cartoon approximation.  
Preserve:  
* Overall head and face shape  
* Eye placement and spacing  
* Brow shape  
* Nose length, width and profile  
* Mouth shape  
* Jawline and cheek structure  
* Hairline and hairstyle  
* Ears  
* Facial hair  
* Age  
* Expression  
* Natural asymmetries  
* Any clearly visible identifying features  
Translate these features into selective flat ink marks and negative space. Use enough internal facial information to hold the likeness, but do not introduce realistic colour, gradients or conventional portrait shading.  
Facial simplification must be selective, not generic. If stylistic simplification and likeness conflict, likeness wins.  
PRESERVE ALL VISIBLE LOGOS AND LETTERING  
Do not remove sponsor logos, brand marks, jersey lettering, player numbers, badges or club insignia.  
The Newcastle Knights crest is a priority identity element and must remain in its correct location, shape, proportion and orientation whenever it is visible in the reference.  
Preserve visible sponsor marks as faithfully as the supplied image allows. Use the reference itself as the source. Do not invent, rename, approximate or replace a sponsor or crest from memory.  
Translate logos and lettering into the same single-colour ink treatment while retaining their recognisable structure. Small marks may be simplified only where the source does not contain enough visible information to reproduce them accurately.  
KEEP THE JERSEY CONSTRUCTION  
Preserve the jersey’s stripes, hoops, chevrons, panels, collar, sleeves and major colour-block structure. Translate different source colours into distinct ink shapes, negative-space gaps or patterns of ink density while remaining within the single-colour system.  
Do not flood the jersey into one undifferentiated block. Its construction, club pattern, logos and crest must remain readable.  
All jersey elements should follow the player’s body, perspective, fabric tension and folds.  
IMPERFECT INK, NOT A DISTRESS FILTER  
Ink coverage should be uneven, slightly starved and physically varied. Use mottling, pinholes, dry-brush gaps, small flecks and areas of heavier ink where pigment appears to pool.  
The breakup must feel specific to the individual marks and shapes. Do not apply a uniform noise texture across the entire illustration.  
Texture should reinforce the direction and pressure of the hand-inked marks rather than sitting over them as a digital effect.  
THE BACKGROUND STAYS CLEAN  
All grain, speckling, broken ink and surface variation belongs inside the printed figure, its internal marks and its outer edges.  
Keep the surrounding off-white negative space clean and unmarked. Do not add paper grain, floating distress, ink splatter or background dirt. The illustration must remain suitable for clean luminance keying and transparent extraction.  
STENCIL INVERSION  
Inversion for dark backgrounds is an export treatment only. Generate the artwork as a standard one-colour ink separation on a clean light ground. Do not ask the model to redesign the illustration as a light-on-dark image.  
FINAL PRIORITY ORDER  
When instructions compete, use this order:  
1. Preserve the exact player’s identity and facial likeness.  
2. Preserve the source pose, action, expression and composition.  
3. Preserve the uniform structure, sponsor marks and club crest.  
4. Maintain organic, observational hand-inked linework.  
5. Translate the image into a single-colour flat ink system.  
6. Apply imperfect screenprint grain within the ink.  
7. Keep the surrounding background completely clean.
